# Part 5. Track your AWS usage

## 33.
In order to track your AWS usage, go to the [AWS Management Console](https://console.aws.amazon.com).

## 34.
Head to your EC2 Dashboard:

![Alt text](image-1.png)

Check “Instances (running)”, and make sure that you do not have any instances running. If you do, you might have forgotten to delete a cluster, so you should run the following command from the Terminal:

```shell
(aws) $ pcluster list-clusters
```

Followed by (replace MyCluster01 below with the name of any clusters you see listed above):

```shell
(aws) $ pcluster delete-cluster -n MyCluster01
```

## 35.
Next, we will check your virtual cpu limits by opening the drop-down menu in the top right and choosing "Service Quotas".

![Alt text](ConsoleQuotas.png)

 which should take you to the following page:

![Alt text](ServiceQuotas.png)

Select "AWS services" in the left pane, type "ec2" in the search bar, and then click on “Amazon Elastic Compute Cloud (Amazon EC2)” in the resulting list:

![Alt text](QuotaServices.png)

Type “running on-demand” in the search bar, and check your Applied quota value for `Running On-Demand Standard (A, C, D, H, I, M, R, T, Z) instances`. If your limit is less than `64`, select that option, and click the `Request quota increase` button in orange in the top right:

![Alt text](EC2Quotas.png)

Enter `64` in the `Change quota value` text box, and then click the `Request` button in the bottom right:

![Alt text](QuotaRequest.png)

## 37.
In order to avoid being surprised by unexpected charges, you can use AWS Budgets to warn you when your credits are running low. First click on "Budget" in the left pane.

![Alt text](BudgetHome.png)

Click on the yellow "Create budget" button. If you select the "Customize (advanced)" option in the "Budget setup" section, and the default "Cost budget - Recommended" in the "Budget types" section:

![Alt text](BudgetType.png)

Scroll down and click the yellow "Next" button. On the new page, scroll down to enter the following information:

![Alt text](Budget1.png)

Scroll further down to enter "400" into the amount:

![Alt text](BudgetAmount.png)

You can scroll to the bottom to click the yellow "Next" button. You should see the following screen.

![Alt text](BudgetAddAlert.png)

You will want to add a few alerts ("Add an alert threshold") to inform you when your total expenses reach 50%, 80%, and 95% of your $400 budget.

![Alt text](BudgetAlert.png)

Once you are done, you should see the following screen:

![Alt text](BudgetNext.png)

You can then click the yellow "Next" button at the bottom, after which you will see the following screen. You can scroll down to the botton to click the yellow "Create budget" button.

![Alt text](CreateBudget.png)

After that, you should be brought back to the budgets dashboard.
Now if you scroll to the right, you should see the column "Current vs. budgeted", which will show you how much you have spent of your $400.

![Alt text](BudgetBar.png)
